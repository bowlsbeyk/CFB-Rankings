I'm a Miami fan. Everyone's mad at the SEC, so I tested it: 11 seasons of AP polls against ratings built only from scores and schedules (no polls, preseason or team names).

Two terms matter. Strength of schedule: how good your opponents were. Strength of record: how hard your record would be for a top-25 team against that schedule. Florida: 3rd-toughest schedule by results, 6–4, record ranks 23rd. Utah: 46th schedule, 10–0, record ranks 1st.

Short version: with the same record, polls still rank SEC teams higher, as they did before NIL.

What the data says:
• Same record, different conference. When an SEC team and a team from another power conference have the same losses and nearly the same strength of record, the AP ranks the SEC team about 2.5 spots higher. An ACC team: about 2.7 lower. The playoff committee does it too.
• Part of it is the preseason poll. Head starts carry over (least clearly for the ACC), and SEC teams start ahead most often.
• Since NIL, that poll premium shows no sign of shrinking: about +1.8 spots in 2014–19, +3.4 since 2021.
• Meanwhile, the SEC's postseason edge has faded: 41–28 (59%) in bowls and the playoff in 2014–19, 30–34 (47%) since 2021, 4–10 last year. The Big Ten went 39–23 and won the last three titles.
• "Ranked wins" pad SEC schedules. This season SEC teams faced a game-day-ranked team 71 times (68 vs SEC rivals) and won 31. 13 of those wins came against teams not top 25 by results today (as did all five ACC ranked wins; the SEC just has more). Texas A&M was #9 when it lost to Kentucky. It's 79th by strength of record now.

What keeps me honest:
• Part of the poll gap is voters being right. Allowing for how the season ended, it's about 1.9 spots up for the SEC and 1.5 down for the ACC.
• By regular-season results alone, the SEC still has the most top-25 teams. But SEC teams mostly play each other, so the regular season says little about a decline (in their few games vs other power conferences, they've done better since 2021).
• ESPN and SP+ rate SEC teams higher than my ratings do.
• The before/after-NIL changes aren't statistically clear over five seasons, and bowls carry opt-outs.
• This was about the seventh test I ran looking for an SEC effect, and the first to find a clear, robust one. Most were logged before they ran, but I picked the headline after seeing them all. Three independent reviewers checked the main test.

Bottom line: judge teams by strength of record, not the logo, the preseason poll or "ranked" wins. During the season, with the same record so far, an SEC team is ranked about two spots higher and an ACC team about two lower. By the final poll the data are too thin to tell, but near the cut line two spots matter.

And Miami? Through week 11 it's #3 by strength of record, with the 22nd-toughest schedule by results (59th by game-day rankings).
