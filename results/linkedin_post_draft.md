I'm a Miami fan. Everyone is mad at the SEC right now, so I tested it: 11 seasons of AP polls against ratings built only from scores and schedules. No polls, no preseason, no team names.

The short version: the polls still treat the SEC like it's 2017.

What the data says:
• Same record, different conference. When an SEC team and a team from another power conference have the same losses and nearly the same strength of record, the AP ranks the SEC team about 2.5 spots higher. The ACC team? About 2.7 spots lower. The playoff committee does it too.
• It starts with the preseason poll. Head starts stick for everyone, and SEC teams start ahead most often.
• Since NIL, that poll premium hasn't shrunk. If anything it grew: about +1.8 spots in 2014–19, +3.4 since 2021.
• Meanwhile, the postseason edge is gone. The SEC went 41–28 (59%) in bowls and the playoff in 2014–19, and 30–34 (47%) since 2021, including 4–10 last year. The Big Ten went 39–23 and won the last three titles.
• "Ranked wins" pad SEC schedules. This season SEC teams have played 71 games against teams ranked on game day and won 31. Thirteen of those wins came against teams that aren't top 25 by results today. Texas A&M was #9 when it lost to Kentucky. It's 79th now.

What keeps me honest:
• Part of the poll gap is voters being right. Allowing for how the season ended, it's about 1.9 spots for the SEC and 1.5 for the ACC. Smaller, but still there.
• By regular-season results alone, the SEC still has the most top-25 teams. But SEC teams mostly play each other, so the regular season can't show a decline the way the postseason can.
• ESPN and SP+ rate SEC teams higher than my ratings do.
• None of the before-and-after-NIL changes is statistically clear over five seasons, and bowls carry opt-outs.
• This was about the seventh test I ran looking for an SEC effect, and the first to find one. Each was written down before it ran, and three independent reviewers checked the core numbers.

Bottom line: judge teams by results, not by the logo or the preseason poll. With the same record, an SEC team starts about two spots up and an ACC team about two down. Near the playoff cut line, two spots can decide who gets in.

And Miami? Through week 11 it's #3 by strength of record, with the 22nd-toughest schedule by how its opponents have actually played. Game-day rankings put that schedule 59th.
